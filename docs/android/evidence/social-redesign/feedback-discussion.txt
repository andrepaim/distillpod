“Feedback is useful when it helps you see the gap between what you tried and what happened.”

The value of feedback is not simply that someone has responded. It is whether that response makes the difference between intention and outcome easier to understand. The excerpt puts that gap at the center of learning, rather than treating feedback as automatically beneficial.

The speaker also emphasizes specificity: an observation should give you something to change on the next attempt. One implication is that useful feedback needs to connect to a decision or behavior. “This did not work” identifies disappointment; “the main point only became clear at the end” offers a more actionable starting point.

The caution about timing, task, and interpretation matters just as much. My reading is that collecting more comments can become a substitute for deciding what to test. Several suggestions may deserve attention, but they do not necessarily deserve attention at once. Choosing one relevant adjustment can make the next attempt easier to evaluate.

In practice, this suggests a simple sequence: clarify what you intended, identify what actually happened, and choose a specific change. Before seeking another round of feedback, it may be worth asking: what will this help me do differently?
